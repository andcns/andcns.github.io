Devlog No. 1 / Systems foundation

Changing the seed now produces a board with branching roads, trees, buildings, different ground surfaces and a river that can cross a road through a bridge. Getting the systems to agree with one another has been the real work behind that result.

What we are building

RomanianPCG is our working name for a procedural puzzle adventure set in the Romanian countryside, built in Unreal Engine 5.8. Structurally inspired by Hand of Fate, it uses a generated board to present branching journeys, encounters and choices. There is no combat: challenge comes from preparation, resources, weather, time and interpreting information. Dialogue, encounters and folklore puzzles still need to become playable.

Giving the board a structure

The current board uses an 8 × 8 hex grid. Each tile has a stable TileID, grid coordinate, world position and route flags. A seeded planner selects start and finish positions, creates a bending main route, then adds branches that split and reconnect. Roads are stored as explicit tile-to-tile connections rather than inferred from nearby meshes. Those connections drive the straight, corner and junction pieces, and will later give gameplay a reliable set of legal moves. Before the board is marked ready, validation checks neighbours, reciprocal connections, reachability, endpoints and disconnected road sections.

Separating the board from its environment

The two main actors are BP_BoardGenerator and BP_BoardEnvironmentGenerator. The former owns the grid and road connections. The latter reads the completed board to build terrain replacements, vegetation, buildings and the river. Keeping these responsibilities apart means a change in tree density does not affect route topology, and a river rebuild does not invalidate the TileIDs needed for gameplay. The environment responds to generation-complete notifications, with manual rebuild available for art iteration. These working systems use Blueprints and instanced meshes; Python is not required at runtime.

Making the seed control the whole result

Environment random streams derive from the board seed, with separate offsets for different systems. This makes it possible to alter scatter rules without consuming road-generation randomness. In a repeatability check I changed seeds through 8, 12, 4 and back to 12; returning to 12 reproduced the arrangement. Editor reconstruction also revealed that a generation counter can reset, while seed comparison misses a rebuild with identical inputs. A fresh generation ID now identifies each completed generation independently of its random result.

Dressing the spaces around the roads

Trees form small clusters on eligible non-road tiles. Candidates must respect road clearance, fit within the tile and keep enough space between trunks on neighbouring tiles. The footprint check also accounts for crowns extending beyond a pivot. Some tiles receive a building instead of a tree cluster, currently using test houses and towers. Placement has bounded attempts, so an oversized or invalid object is skipped rather than forcing a result. Density, scale, spacing and clearance are exposed art controls. These objects remain decorative for now.

Letting terrain respond to the layout

The ground now forms readable regions. Road surfaces remain stone, river neighbours tend toward grass, and other tiles vary between grass and dirt using a seeded spatial field. Immediate road and river neighbourhoods examine the six valid hex neighbours. This replaced a repeatedly scanning distance-based approach that became costly in the combined Blueprint generation flow and contributed to loop-limit errors. Grass and dirt meshes also had mismatched heights. Adjusting the vertical scale from their mesh bounds now produces a consistent 120-unit terrain depth.

Building one continuous river

The river planner chooses a complete route from one board boundary to another, comparing straighter options with bends under road-crossing constraints. Incoming and outgoing directions select and rotate compatible river tiles. A suitable road segment can become a bridge made from two ramps and a raised deck; there is at most one crossing, and some seeds have none. I have physically walked the character across a generated bridge in Play mode, although that is not yet a finished board-navigation system.

Fixing the hexes underneath

Early river tests showed overlapping surfaces: replacement terrain and original stone hexes were both visible. A missed regeneration refresh had brought back the underlying board mesh. The generation ID corrected that. Logical tiles retain their IDs, while the original visible instance is suppressed whenever the environment supplies a replacement; instance slots remain stable to avoid shifting index mappings. One seed-18 check showed 64 logical tiles, 36 original visible surfaces and 28 replacements, with no overlap. Clearing restored all 64 originals. A later bridge rotation bug came from restoring a zero-scale mesh transform; road orientation is now recovered from logical connection data.

Testing the rebuilds

The board has a recorded 37-seed validation pass. Environment testing has covered twelve seeds, repeatability, river continuity, terrain ownership, depth and bridge behaviour. Further tests covered same-seed regeneration, seed changes, board radius changes and manual art rebuilds. Collision traces hit the new terrain surfaces instead of hidden stone tiles. Refresh guards also reduced repeated environment rebuilds to one per completed board generation. A packaged build and the complete player board-travel system remain unverified.

Where the project goes next

The current visuals use Kenney hex-kit assets and Epic Top Down template content as placeholders. They are useful for testing silhouettes, scale and composition, but the houses and towers are not the final Romanian village kit. Our visual direction is a low-poly retro countryside with readable silhouettes, low-resolution textures and researched architecture and folklore. The next priority is a complete playable run: route choices, dialogue and modifiers, basic resources and inventory, and a folklore-driven playable biome with weather affecting puzzle logic. I own art direction, environment art and PCG/technical-art systems; Abi and Robert own gameplay programming and integration, while Vlad owns music, sound and atmosphere.

We now have a countryside board that can rebuild coherently. The next milestone is giving players a reason to study its routes, prepare for what lies ahead and make a decision that matters.
